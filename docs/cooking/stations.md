# The Four Stations

Cooking happens at furniture you own and place yourself. There are four stations, each doing a
different job, and most dishes pass through more than one of them.

Buy them from the **Cooking** section of the server shop, or ask **Kate** — she keeps them in stock.

| Station | What it does | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prep Station** | Shaped recipes: sandwiches, bowls, sushi, doughs | 25,000 |
| **Chopping Station** | Dicing and juicing raw produce | 15,000 |
| **Kitchen Stove** | Timed cooking: stews, fries, curries, meats | 50,000 |
| **Kitchen Oven** | Timed baking: breads, cakes, pastries, jellies | 85,000 |

## Prep Station

The kitchen island. Right-click it and it asks what you're making:

- **Small Meal** — a 3×3 board
- **Large Meal** — a 4×4 board

Both live in the same piece of furniture, so you only ever need one.

Prep recipes are **shaped**: where you put each ingredient matters, not just what you have. The
layout is part of what you're discovering, and it's shown in the recipe book once you know a dish.
The board can hold the shape anywhere on it — you don't have to hug a corner.

!!! tip "Let the book fill the board"
    Open the recipe book at a prep station, pick a recipe, and press **Move Ingredients**. It pulls
    everything out of your inventory and lays it out correctly. Much faster than placing nine items
    by hand, and it can't get the shape wrong.

## Chopping Station

The kitchen counter. One ingredient in, a processed one out — diced vegetables, juices, pastes.

Chopping is the one station with a **minigame**. A marker sweeps across a track and you click while
it's inside the green window. Land it and the cut counts; land four and the job's done.

You always have a choice:

| How | Yield | Experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Time the cuts** | Full | Full |
| **Quick chop** | Much less | Much less |

The quick chop exists because timing forty cucumbers is tiring. Use it when you're processing bulk
and take the timed cut when the ingredient is worth it.

A **knife** in your inventory raises the yield by half again — see [Knives & Tools](tools.md).

## Kitchen Stove and Kitchen Oven

The two timed stations. Load the ingredients, start it, and wait. A floating readout above the
station shows a progress bar and, depending on your Cooking level, a countdown.

!!! warning "Food burns"
    When a dish finishes it does **not** wait for you indefinitely. Leave it too long and it burns —
    you get a ruined item, or nothing at all. Stay nearby and collect it.

    A higher Cooking level buys you a longer grace period, on top of a clearer timer.

Surplus is fine. A stack of flour bakes one loaf and keeps the rest, the way a furnace smelts one
ore at a time — you don't have to count out exact amounts. What the station *won't* accept is an
ingredient the recipe has no use for.

The stove handles savoury cooking: stews, soups, fried and grilled meats, curries, drinks. The oven
handles everything baked: breads, cakes, pastries, ice creams and jellies. The oven is the larger
investment and it holds the bulk of the high-rarity menu.

## Owning a station

Stations are yours. You buy the furniture, place it wherever you like, and break it to pick it back
up.

- **Private by default.** Only you can cook at a station you placed.
- **You can open it up.** Toggle a station public and anyone may use it — handy for a guild kitchen
  or a town square.
- **Some stations are public already.** Staff-placed stations around the world belong to nobody and
  are open to everyone. If you're not ready to buy, look for one of those.
- **One cook at a time.** A station in use is a station in use. Walk up to a busy oven and you'll be
  told to wait your turn.

That last rule matters more than it sounds, because it also applies to [bots](../automation/bots.md):
a cooking bot has to queue for the station like anybody else, and it loses to a player.
