    Brewing & The Essence Almanac¶

    Brewing is cooking with the recipe book thrown away. There are no drink recipes: every fruit and vegetable carries a hidden Essence — a stat it pushes — and a few weaker Undertones behind it. You pour whatever you like into a base, and the mix decides what comes out.

    The machine for it is the Drink Machine, sold in the Cooking section of the server shop (ask Kate). Place it like any other station — the same ownership and one-at-a-time rules apply.

    How a brew works¶

    1. Put a base in the top slot. The base is the vessel and the ceiling: it decides how many ingredient slots you get, how many buffs the drink can hold, and how strong they may get.
    2. Fill the slots with any brewing ingredients — crops, eggs, honeycomb, frog legs. No grid, no layout, no exact amounts. More of the same ingredient pushes its stat harder (with diminishing returns), and silver and gold star produce pushes it much harder.
    3. Watch the preview. It shows exactly what the current mix will make — but only for ingredients you've learned (see the Almanac below). Unknown ingredients still work at full strength; the preview just marks them ?.
    4. Brew. Instant — no timer, no burning. The base and every ingredient are consumed.

    Every drink restores health and mana on top of its buffs, and drinks run long and gentle — the sipping counterpart to food. One drink buff set is active at a time; drinking something new replaces it. Drink buffs stack with food buffs and potion buffs, so being fully buffed means all three.

    Bases¶

    Base Slots Max buffs Where from
    Water Bottle 3 2 vanilla — free
    Milk 4 2 vanilla (bonus duration)
    Honey Bottle 5 2 your bees (bonus potency)
    Tea Base 6 3 Cooking shop
    Ale Base 7 3 Cooking shop
    Cider Base 8 3 Cooking shop
    Wine Base 10 4 Cooking shop

    Better bases also raise how strong a buff can get. A water bottle brews you something honest; a wine base is where the serious drinks happen.

    The Essence Almanac¶

    Open it with /almanac. Every brewing ingredient has a page, and the pages fill in as you play:

    Stage How What it shows
    Hidden never seen it a silhouette
    Discovered pick the ingredient up its name and a hint of its character
    Tasted brew with it once its Essence — the stat it pushes
    Understood brew with it five times its Undertones and the real numbers

    The wiki won't spoil it

    Which ingredient carries which Essence is deliberately not documented here. Learning that lychees do that is the profession. Experiment, fill your Almanac, trade what you know.

    Undertones are the deep end: they're too weak to matter alone, but ingredients from the same family reinforce each other, and a mix built on those overlaps quietly earns a buff you never explicitly added. Noticing those combinations is what separates a brewer from someone with a blender.

    Fine, Superior, Masterwork¶

    Every brew rolls once against your Cooking level — and it only ever rolls up:

    • Fine brew — lasts noticeably longer.
    • Superior brew — one buff jumps a whole tier.
    • Masterwork — a tier jump and a hidden undertone surfaces as a bonus buff. Labelled on the bottle, and worth real money to the right buyer.

    A failed roll simply gives you exactly what the preview promised. No brew is ever ruined.

    Signature drinks¶

    A handful of exact combinations produce a named drink with its own look and a little extra strength — Elderberry Wine, Mulled Cider, Spiced Ale, Cinnamon Brew and more. There is no list; hitting one is the discovery. If your brew suddenly has a name, write the mix down.

    Beer and Wine from the old stove days live on here: the old recipes are gone, and the drink machine is where all drinking now begins.
